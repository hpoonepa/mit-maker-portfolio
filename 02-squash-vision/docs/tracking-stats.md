# Tracking estimates

`GET /api/v1/matches/{id}/reports/{revision}/tracking-stats` derives descriptive statistics from the selected report's saved observations. It returns the report/prediction revision and the separately applied calibration revision. `calibrationRevision` can pin a saved calibration; otherwise the latest calibration for the same proxy is used. This does not rerun inference or rewrite published report metrics.

The coordinate origin is the **front-left floor corner**. X increases across the court's 6.40 m width; Y increases toward the rear wall over 9.75 m. The WSF specification places the nearest edge of the short line 4.26 m from the rear wall and gives all lines a 50 mm width. That makes its rear edge Y=5.49 m and its center Y=5.465 m. Manual image landmarks must consistently identify the chosen edge or center. See [World Squash, technical specifications, Appendix 7](https://worldsquashofficiating.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Rules-of-Singles-Squash-2020-V2.pdf).

**Player position at contact** uses existing racket-contact candidates inferred from ball, pose, and available audio evidence. A location is the inferred hitter's observed feet position within 80 ms of the contact, projected onto the court floor. It is not a measured racket/ball impact point. Unknown hitters stay unknown; ambiguous, interpolated, outside-court, or high-uncertainty player positions have no coordinate. Classified/corrected shot timestamps and hitters take precedence for matching contact evidence. Contact video links use the exact source-frame-to-proxy-frame index.

**Tracked movement** sums each A/B track's valid floor path. Three-point coordinate medians and 200 ms sampling reduce ankle jitter. Ambiguous/interpolated samples, association confidence below 0.5, estimated floor uncertainty above 0.75 m, outside-court positions, gaps over 120 ms, and intervals above 12 m/s are omitted. The speed threshold is a tracking-jump guard, not a measured player ability. Reported seconds/coverage show retained intervals; the distance is a partial observed path, not an extrapolated full-match total. A/B labels remain unverified and identity switches can still affect estimates.

**Estimated ball speed** is apparent displacement after projecting image ball centers onto the floor plane. It uses consecutive source frames no more than 100 ms apart, observed visible unambiguous detections, confidence at least 0.5, one candidate, and positions inside the court projection. The median and 90th percentile are returned. With at least eight intervals, a reproducible upper fence of Q3 + 3×IQR removes extreme outliers. Excluded intervals and retained time coverage are reported.

A single camera's floor homography does not recover an airborne ball's depth/height. This speed can overestimate or underestimate actual 3D speed and is **not racket-launch speed**. Missing calibration or usable observations produces an unavailable value, never a fabricated zero. A genuine stationary observed player path can produce zero metres.

Artifact paths, sizes, and SHA-256 hashes are checked before reading the same owned file descriptor. Overlapping chunk observations are deduplicated. Calibration can be applied to an older report even after newer calibration invalidates mutable job-chunk status; the selected report's saved artifacts remain its source.

Coordinate scaling uses the actual emitted inference dimensions recorded with ball observations. FAST/BALANCED player-only jobs use saved proxy-calibration dimensions. Historical MAXIMUM player-only jobs with ambiguous high-resolution/rotated pixel provenance return unavailable instead of assuming a scale. This avoids confusing original 4K pixels with the bounded proxy pixels used by newer inference runs.

Validation: 36 focused mathematical/API tests and the existing OpenAPI contract check passed. They cover known-distance and stationary paths, exclusions and gaps, exact proxy seek times, known projected speed, unavailable calibration, explicit report/calibration selection, artifact tampering, and high-resolution/rotated and player-only coordinate handling. Actual demo estimates are saved in `runtime/evidence/tracking-stats.json`; these are not an accuracy benchmark.

## Synchronized playback series

`GET /api/v1/matches/{id}/reports/{revision}/tracking-timeline?startUs=0&endUs=120000000` returns at most 120 seconds of source-time samples. It shares the movement calculation with the totals endpoint; live speed uses the distinct 2D method below. Per-player baselines preserve accepted movement before the requested window, so seeking ahead does not reset distance or reveal later movement early. Cumulative distance changes only at an accepted interval's endpoint. Missing or ambiguous positions remain unavailable.

Each sample includes its source frame and exact proxy display start/end from the timestamp index. Current ball speed is the clean interval **ending at that displayed frame**, not a future interval or the full-clip median. Every live interval uses `court_scaled_image_speed`: image displacement per second multiplied by 6.40 m divided by projected court width at the interval's midpoint image Y. That Y is clamped to the visible front/rear floor extent, so airborne pixels above the floor use the front-court width. This is an apparent 2D image-motion estimate, not recovered 3D speed. The static totals retain their separate floor-projected median method. The UI must hide it after the supplied display interval instead of retaining the last successful estimate across tracking gaps.

Temporal impact candidates are derived separately from the immutable report's original classifications. A racket candidate can have a ball image position and a separate player feet position. A floor-bounce candidate can have a projected ball floor position. Neither a racket candidate nor a wall candidate may borrow player feet or a floor projection as a ball impact coordinate. Front-wall schematic fractions are normalized image positions, not metric wall measurements; metric wall coordinates remain unavailable without separate wall calibration. Candidates retain their timing uncertainty, inference provenance, and review requirement.

Contact lists and landing counts describe the requested window. They are sparse estimated observations, not a complete or verified match shot count. Playback reveals these saved/derived observations in time; it does not run the neural models live.

The playback impact estimator uses five reliable observed ball centers, with no more than one missing source frame between supports. It requires an absolute change in image velocity of at least 0.10 image diagonals per second and a relative change of at least 0.35. This rejects smooth, slow flight apexes that the older direction-only candidates could confuse with contacts. Adjacent image speeds above 3.5 diagonals per second are rejected as likely tracking jumps; peaks within 160 ms are consolidated.

Racket candidates combine a sharp speed increase with nearby observed wrists and estimated racket reach. Floor candidates require a calibrated floor position and a downward image trajectory that slows or reverses. Front-wall candidates require a sharp change above the calibrated front-floor edge; their horizontal marker is a normalized image fraction. These are physical heuristics, not a validated contact classifier. Perspective, occlusion, racket reach, and missed observations can still misclassify or miss impacts. Every derived impact stays estimated and review-required. Timing uncertainty spans its supporting observations, typically 80 ms in the 25 fps demo.

Seven physical regressions cover apex rejection, floor coordinates, racket/floor separation, unavailable wall height, calibration loss, occlusion/reacquisition rejection, and image scaling. The actual before/at/after frames for four floor-bounce candidates are preserved in `runtime/evidence/floor-bounce-frame-check.jpg`; these spot checks do not establish overall accuracy.

## Per-player impact attribution

`shotHitter`, `shotContactId`, and `shotHitterConfidence` distinguish the inferred owner of a shot from the direct hitter of an individual event. A racket candidate with selected A/B and hitter support at least 0.35 starts ownership. A later floor/front-wall candidate can inherit that owner for at most 3 seconds, only within the same observed ball continuity segment. Unknown racket/unknown impact candidates clear ownership. No future hitter is used to fill an earlier unknown event.

Continuity uses clean observed ball samples at most 120 ms apart, increasing source-frame indices, consistent image/source provenance, and a 3.5-image-diagonals/second jump guard. Invalid/interpolated rows are omitted and cannot fill longer gaps; this does not require every source frame to be visible. Ordered impact history is processed before response-window filtering, so a known earlier racket can own a surface event near a window boundary. Overlap observations at the same timestamp/player prefer actual observed evidence over interpolation.

These fields remain estimates, and A/B tracking switches or false/missed impacts can misattribute later marks. The demo has 18 racket candidates: 4 assigned A, 9 assigned B, 5 unassigned. This is not a validated shot count and must not be presented as one. Counts of marked landings describe displayed candidates, not verified rally totals.
