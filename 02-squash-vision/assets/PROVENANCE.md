# Demo media provenance

## Current portfolio demo, 31 October 2026

`squashvision-demo.mp4` is the edited recording of the current application reviewing an ElShorbagy vs Farag Canary Wharf rally. `review-demo.png` and `shot-review.png` are frames from that recording. It combines saved model tracking with separately reviewed shot annotations; the placement ratings are estimates from a rubric. It is not a tracking-accuracy or live-inference benchmark.

The professional footage corresponds to SQUASHTV’s [“The quality is just SO HIGH!” — ElShorbagy v Farag — Free Game Friday](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y0Y0j_sih1A), from the 2020 SJP Canary Wharf Classic final. Local preparation records identify source seconds **250–275**. The original download URL was not retained; the linked official upload matches the players, event and Free Game title. Broadcast copyright remains with SQUASHTV/its rights holders, separate from the application code. See [citations](../CITATIONS.md).

`personal-rally.png` is from Harsha's own match recording, `IMG_4285.mov`. It establishes the personal context for the project and is not the match used in the app demonstration.

The recordings and figures are submitted separately with the portfolio. The [project citations](../CITATIONS.md) credit their sources and the tracking models.

## Earlier development fixtures

## `tracking-overlay.gif` / `tracking-overlay-still.png`

Produced by running this repository's real models over the first 100 frames
(4.0 s) of `tests/squashvision/fixtures/squash-review-real-model.mp4`.

- **Pose:** Ultralytics YOLO11n-pose, the exact checkpoint this project pins
  (`sha256 869e83fc…19dc0`, 6,255,593 bytes), loaded through
  `PlayerDetector` with its normal content-address check. Ran on MPS.
- **Ball:** TrackNetV3, the exact pinned checkpoint
  (`sha256 00d707b9…72342`, 46,585,585 bytes), verified against
  `TRUSTED_BALL_REVISION` / `TRUSTED_BALL_SHA256` / `TRUSTED_BALL_BYTE_SIZE`
  and loaded with `torch.load(weights_only=True)`. Ran on CPU.
- **Identity:** `PlayerIdentityTracker` with its production defaults and no
  calibration supplied, so no floor-distance term and no metric output.

Counts from that run: **198 player observations over 100 frames**
(two slots per frame, `evidence=observed`), **97 ball rows of which 67 are
`visible`**. The remaining 30 are frames where the tracker declined to select a
point. The first three frames have no ball row at all because TrackNetV3 needs a
four-frame input window.

Every overlay element is read from the model output — bounding boxes, COCO-17
keypoints, the `A`/`B` slot assignment, `association_confidence`, the ball point,
its probability and its candidate count. Nothing in the image is drawn from a
fixture or a hand-written value.

### What this does and does not show

The fixture is **one real squash rally frame repeated into a 20-second clip**,
with a small patch from the same source animated along a scripted path and
synthetic impact audio added. So:

- The pose detections are real inference on real squash footage.
- The players do not move, because the frame is looped. This demonstrates the
  detector and the identity assignment, **not** tracking through motion or
  through occlusion.
- The ball track follows a scripted synthetic patch, not a real ball.
- **None of this measures accuracy.** There is no ground truth here. See the
  README's Results section.

## `squash_fixture.mp4` / `squash_fixture_vfr.mp4`

Fully synthetic clips, nominally generated by `scripts/make_squash_fixture.py` —
a coloured background, drawn court lines, two rectangles standing in for players
and a square standing in for the ball, plus a deterministic click track. They
exist to exercise media ingest, the constant- versus variable-frame-rate
timestamp mapping, and the chunking paths. They contain no people, so the pose
model finds nothing in them; that much is intentional.

### These two files have drifted from their generator

The committed clips do **not** match what `make_squash_fixture.py` draws.
Measured over all 125 frames of `squash_fixture.mp4`:

| Generator specifies | Committed file contains |
|---|---|
| player A box, `color=0x42a5f5`, translating `x = 130 + 150·t` | **zero pixels** within L1 distance 60 of that colour, in any frame; nearest colour present is L1 201 away |
| player B box, `color=0xff7043`, translating `x = 1050 − 145·t` | **zero pixels**, nearest L1 124 away |
| 13×13 white ball on `x = 620 + 300·sin(2πt/1.25)`, `y = 320 + 130·cos(…)` | a 169-px (13×13) white blob, **frozen at (449, 220) in every frame** |
| court lines and background | present and correct |

Frame-to-frame change is a median mean-absolute difference of 0.13/255, with the
differing pixels spread evenly over the whole court bounding box — that is h264
noise on a static image, not a 600-pixel sinusoidal sweep and two boxes
translating across the frame.

**Consequence:** these clips could never have exercised pose detection or ball
tracking, whatever the generator's filter chain implies. With no motion at all,
TrackNetV3's four-frame differencing input is degenerate.

**What is not affected:** the backend test suite does not read these files. The
`squash_fixtures` fixture in `tests/squashvision/backend/test_media_ingest.py` shells out to
`scripts/make_squash_fixture.py` and generates fresh clips into a temp directory
on every run, so the tests exercise the generator's real current output. The
drift is confined to these two committed copies, which were carried over from an
old demo output directory rather than regenerated.

They are kept as-is rather than regenerated because
`court-calibration-fixture-run.png` was computed against these exact bytes, and
replacing them would make that figure describe a file no longer in the
repository.

## `court-calibration-fixture-run.png`

Produced by running this repository's `detect_court()` and `fit_calibration()`
(`src/squashvision/backend/squashvision/calibration.py`) over every frame of both committed
fixtures — 500 frames of `tests/squashvision/fixtures/squash-review-real-model.mp4` and 125
of `assets/squash_fixture.mp4`, 625 frames in total.

Every number in the two tables and both plots is a literal per-frame return
value of those two functions: line-segment count, intersection count, proposal
confidence, corner-correspondence count, the warning string, and wall-clock
runtime. Nothing is fitted, smoothed or illustrative, and no frame is selected
for being flattering — the displayed frames are representative of the whole run,
which declined on all 625.

The calibrator returned `calibrated=False` on every frame of both clips:
`insufficient_orientation_evidence` on the real footage (median ~3 line
segments) and `missing_internal_court_topology` on the synthetic one, both
ending at `0 of 4` required corner correspondences and therefore
`insufficient_floor_correspondences` from `fit_calibration`.

This figure covers the calibration stage only. Pose and ball tracking are shown
in `tracking-overlay.gif` above.
