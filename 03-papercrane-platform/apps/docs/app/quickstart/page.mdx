# Quickstart

A complete binder design run, end to end, in about five minutes. We'll target IL-7Rα as a
worked example, but any PDB ID or AlphaFold accession works.

## 1. Sign up (30 seconds)

Open [app.papercrane.bio](https://app.papercrane.bio) and sign up with email or GitHub.
You start on the free tier — 50 seeds, two concurrent jobs.

## 2. Create a project (20 seconds)

A **project** is your namespace for related work — targets, jobs, designs, and team
members all live inside one. From the dashboard, click **New project** and name it
`il7r-binders`.

## 3. Add a target (1 minute)

Inside your project, click **Add target** and paste a PDB ID:

```
3DI3
```

Paper Crane fetches the structure, splits chains, and asks you which chain you want to
bind. Pick chain `A` and select hotspots — either let the platform infer them (default)
or click residues directly on the 3D viewer.

## 4. Launch a job (10 seconds)

Click **New job** → **Binder design (default)**. The default pipeline is:

1. **RFdiffusion** — generate 200 backbones around your hotspots
2. **ProteinMPNN** — assign sequences to each backbone
3. **Boltz-2** — predict the binder/target complex and score it
4. **ESM-2** — pre-filter on pseudo-log-likelihood

Click **Run**. The job lands in the queue and starts within ~30 seconds.

## 5. Watch it run (~3 minutes)

The job page streams live logs and shows per-tool progress. Each design becomes a row in
the table as soon as Boltz-2 finishes scoring it. Sort by `ipsae` (Paper Crane's
recommended composite metric — see [Designs & metrics](/concepts/designs-and-metrics)).

## 6. Download designs (10 seconds)

Click the top design's row → **Download bundle**. You get:

- The designed sequence (FASTA)
- The predicted complex (CIF + PDB)
- A metrics JSON with ipTM, pAE, pLDDT, ipSAE, dG estimate
- A reproducibility manifest (tool versions, seeds, params)

```bash
papercrane jobs download <job-id> --top 10 --out ./designs/
```

That bundle is what you order from a peptide synthesizer. The metrics manifest is what
your collaborator reads when they ask "but is it real?".
