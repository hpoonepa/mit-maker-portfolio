   04 / Paste-ready abstract and results
   Suggested revised abstract
   Specializing a molecular simulation model to an observed endpoint can improve prediction while
   changing its ensemble behavior. We study source-anchored exact-lag adaptation: fine-tune MarS-FM
   on same-replica mdCATH frame pairs separated by 50 saved frames, then retain 7.35% of the learned
   weight displacement. Original validation artifacts report a 0.03259 Å reduction in ensemble-mean
   endpoint RMSD across 28 eligible proteins, positive compact-panel effects from three trained parents,
   and passage of seven source-relative retention screens. We complement these artifacts with a new
   frozen-checkpoint evaluation that separates endpoint accuracy, predictive spread, and
   distribution-sensitive scoring. On eight previously used validation proteins, a prefix-preserving
   extension from K=4 to K=16 retains lower endpoint RMSD on every protein, while the mean gain
   decreases from 0.02401 to 0.01383 Å. At K=16, the statistical energy-score gain is 0.00642 Å
   (conditional protein-resampling interval 0.00160-0.01166 Å). Both aligned-C-alpha spread summaries
   decrease. These results show that small adaptation gains can survive increased sampling, but their
   magnitude and interpretation depend on ensemble size and metric choice. The contribution is a
   sampling-aware evaluation of conservative adaptation, not a new architecture or evidence of
   calibrated physical kinetics. All evidence remains validation-only at 450 K and one lag.

   New Results subsection: Sampling-aware frozen-checkpoint evaluation
   We evaluated the fixed selected checkpoint with a newly implemented evaluator on eight preselected,
   previously used validation proteins (160 transitions). This evaluation is distinct from the original
   28-protein result and does not reproduce its unavailable evaluator. At K=4, protein-macro centroid
   RMSD decreased from 11.05817 to 11.03417 Å. Extending the same ensembles to K=16 preserved
   lower endpoint error on all eight proteins, with source and candidate errors of 10.04244 and 10.02861
   Å (gain 0.01383 Å; conditional interval 0.00733-0.02194 Å). The gain was 42.4% smaller than at K=4.
   The K=16 statistical energy score decreased from 6.84525 to 6.83883 Å; seven of eight proteins
   improved. Ensemble variance and pairwise RMSD decreased by 1.87020 Å² and 0.11302 Å. Thus the
   endpoint advantage survives larger ensembles and is accompanied by a favorable aggregate
   distribution-sensitive score, but smaller spread is not itself proof of improved diversity or calibration.
   Figures 1-2 show the nested sampling comparison and every protein, including the energy-score
   regression.

   Contribution statement for the introduction
   We contribute (i) a transparent source-anchored adaptation study with explicit failure cases; (ii) an
   evaluation that distinguishes endpoint error, finite-ensemble variance, and statistical energy score;
   and (iii) a prefix-preserving sampling analysis showing that endpoint gains persist but diminish as
   ensemble size increases. The contribution is empirical and evaluative; linear interpolation and the
   scoring rules are established methods.

   05 / Methods and exact edits to main.pdf
   New methods subsection - use with the new Results text
   The fixed eight-domain panel contains 1e7uA04, 1rquA01, 1q16B03, 1d1rA00, 2k4qA00, 2f2cA02,
   5k2mA01 and 2dp9A01. For a replica with N frames, starts are floor(i × (N - 51) / 3), i = 0, 1, 2, 3;
   targets are 50 saved frames later. All five 450 K replicas are used. Hash-verified HDF5 inputs are
   mapped to complete atom14 heavy-atom structures in float32 angstroms. Source and candidate use
   ten ODE steps and identical paired random streams. Targets and each sample are independently
   Kabsch-aligned to the starting C-alpha coordinates, never to the future target.

   For transition index j = 0,...,19, the original K=4 seed is 20260904 + j. Three extra K=4 blocks use
   that seed plus b × 1,000,000, b = 1, 2, 3. K=8 joins blocks 0 and 1; K=16 joins all four blocks. Original
   samples are preserved exactly. The extension was specified before its outputs, after the initial K=4
   results; the panel was never changed. Means first average 20 transitions within protein, then weight
   eight proteins equally. Intervals use 2,000 protein bootstrap resamples with seed 20260904. These
   nested estimates do not add proteins or training seeds.

   The statistical energy score is mean sample-to-target RMS Euclidean distance minus one half of the
   mean off-diagonal sample-pair RMS Euclidean distance. Use unsquared distances. Sample MSE =
   centroid MSE + ensemble variance, where ensemble variance has denominator K. The finite-K
   mean-error diagnostic is centroid MSE - ensemble variance/(K-1), under iid forecast draws. It corrects
   model Monte Carlo variance against one observed target, not target uncertainty or biological bias.

   What is supported, and what is still missing
   Completed: limited eight-protein endpoint/decomposition/spread/energy evaluation and the K=4/8/16
   sampling component. Not newly run: other training seeds, a conservative-training comparator, or an
   alpha/retention sweep. The original paper already reports earlier seed and retention artifacts; those
   can remain labeled as earlier evidence, but were not independently rerun here. All new results
   concern one released seed, one lag and temperature, and transformed C-alpha coordinates. No
   untouched test-set or experimental-data validation was performed.

   Future potential - present as hypotheses
   The next research question is whether inexpensive adaptation can improve application-relevant
   observables on a fresh evaluation boundary. Priorities are matched conservative-training controls,
   independent trained seeds, untouched sequence-family validation, and temperature/lag transfer. A
   future motion-aware pocket model could test whether generated motion adds value beyond an
   apo-structure-only baseline. No pocket predictor, binding gain, physiological relevance or
   experimental improvement is established by these results.
