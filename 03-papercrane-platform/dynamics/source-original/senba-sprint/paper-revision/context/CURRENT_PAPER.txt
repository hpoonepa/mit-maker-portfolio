                                                      Abstract

1             Exact-lag adaptation targets observed endpoints, but it may erode ensemble behav-
2             ior inherited from the source model. We study a deliberately small intervention:
3             ﬁne-tune all parameters of M AR S-FM on same-replica mdCATH frame pairs sep-
4             arated by exactly 50 frames, then interpolate the resulting weights back toward
5             the pinned source checkpoint. The selected validation-stage checkpoint retains
6             only 7.35% of the learned displacement. On the complete eligible panel of 28
7             previously inspected mdCATH validation proteins, it lowers the primary error—
8             protein-macro RMSD of the ensemble-mean Cα endpoint—by 0.03259 Å relative
9             to source M AR S-FM (protein-bootstrap 95% interval [0.02525, 0.04011]; 27/28
10            proteins improve) and by 0.43641 Å relative to coordinate persistence ([0.20303,
11            0.68065]; 22/28 improve). On an eight-protein compact panel, every protein im-
12            proves over source for each of three independently trained parents. A separately
13            frozen eight-protein suite meets prespeciﬁed gates for all seven ensemble observ-
14            ables, but these thresholds are development criteria rather than equivalence tests.
15            We also report contrary evidence: more aggressive adaptation failed retention
16            screens, a nearby 7.5% anchor failed its one-protein pilot, and one seed did not
17            improve on an earlier anchor. All evidence is validation-only at this single 50-
18            frame lag. No standard-test record was opened (test_records_opened=0). No
19            physical-clock, ﬁeld-baseline, broad-generalization, or completed-model conclu-
20            sion is supported.



21   1 Introduction

22   Molecular simulation foundation models offer a route to amortizing expensive sampling across pro-
23   teins. The source model considered here, M AR S-FM, generates molecular conformations by learn-
24   ing transitions across states supplied by a Markov-state construction rather than directly ﬁtting one
25   ﬁxed temporal offset [Kapuśniak et al., 2025]. That objective is useful for broad conformational
26   generation, but it need not optimize prediction of a particular observed displacement. Conversely,
27   direct supervision on frame pairs at one lag can specialize the model to that endpoint task and move
28   it away from source behavior that is valuable for broader ensemble sampling.
29   This work asks a narrow development question: can a small, explicitly source-anchored weight
30   update improve exact-lag endpoint prediction without breaching prespeciﬁed ensemble-retention
31   limits? The setting is mdCATH, a collection of all-atom molecular dynamics trajectories for protein
32   domains with multiple replicas and temperatures [Mirarchi et al., 2024]. We use only a frozen
33   128-domain training subset and 32-domain validation subset at 450 K. The selected candidate is
34   evaluated on every validation domain eligible under the source model’s 256-residue crop, not on the
35   standard test partition.

36   The intervention separates learning from evaluation. First, a parent model is ﬁne-tuned on observed
37   same-replica transitions. Second, the evaluated checkpoint is a point on the line segment from
38   the source to the parent. Weight interpolation has been used in other transfer settings to balance
39   adaptation and source robustness [Wortsman et al., 2022]; here it is not treated as a general guarantee.
40   Its role is a transparent control on parameter displacement whose value must be justiﬁed by the
41   molecular validation gates.
42   The contribution is evidence rather than a new foundation architecture. Speciﬁcally, we provide: (i)
43   an exact-lag adaptation and source-anchoring procedure with a ﬁxed data boundary; (ii) a complete
44   eligible validation-panel comparison to the pinned source and coordinate persistence; (iii) compact
45   replication across three independently trained parents; (iv) a seven-observable ensemble-retention
46   screen; and (v) negative results and a machine-checkable claim ledger. The central result is small:
47   the candidate improves the primary endpoint metric by roughly 0.03 Å relative to the source on this
48   panel. The design intentionally makes that modest effect, and the substantial evidence still missing,
49   visible.


50   2 Evidence boundary and study design

51   2.1   Frozen roles

52   The adaptation subset contains 128 train domains and 32 validation domains. Only training domains
53   update weights. Validation domains determine checkpoint and interpolation choices and support
54   every result in this paper. Earlier source-anchoring work had already inspected the compact panel
55   and the complete eligible validation population before the present ten-epoch reﬁnement. The study is
56   therefore retrospective development evidence, not a pristine prospective validation. The standard test
57   partition remained closed, and every cited experimental receipt records test_records_opened=0.
58   All transition supervision and evaluation in this study use the 450 K mdCATH trajectories. The
59   source dataset supplies ﬁve replicas per protein. Exact-lag pairs remain within a replica: for a start
60   index t, the target is t + 50. We retain the lag in frame units. Although mdCATH records simulation
61   metadata, this experiment neither calibrates a physical clock nor measures rates or long-timescale
62   kinetics.
63   The complete endpoint panel is deﬁned by manifest order and the source model’s crop: all 28 valida-
64   tion domains of at most 256 residues are included. No protein is removed after outcome inspection.
65   The compact replication and equilibrium-retention panels contain the ﬁrst eight eligible validation
66   proteins. These nested panels serve different purposes: the complete panel measures the selected
67   seed-1 endpoint effect, the compact panel tests training-seed repeatability at bounded cost, and the
68   equilibrium panel screens whether source ensemble observables are retained.

69   2.2   Source and baselines

70   The source model is M AR S-FM, introduced by Kapuśniak et al. [2025]; its implementation and
71   checkpoint are locally byte-pinned for this study. The candidate keeps the source architecture, infer-
72   ence procedure, and parameter count. Two baselines are matched on exactly the same observed tran-
73   sitions: source M AR S-FM and coordinate persistence, which predicts the starting Cα coordinates
74   as the endpoint. No other ﬁeld method was run under this split and sampling contract. Best-of-four
75   sampling error is computed as a diagnostic, but it is not a primary result because choosing a sample
76   using the unknown future endpoint is unavailable at inference time.

77   2.3   Development sequence and leakage control

78   The development sequence matters for interpreting uncertainty. A coarse epoch and source-anchor
79   search used previously inspected validation evidence. Retention pilots then rejected candidates that
80   moved too far from the source. After choosing the seed-1 checkpoint, the complete endpoint panel,
81   compact seed replications, and eight-protein retention aggregate were frozen as validation artifacts.
82   The reported protein-bootstrap intervals quantify resampling variability within those panels; they do
83   not undo hyperparameter selection on validation data and must not be read as independent conﬁrma-
84   tory intervals.

90    3 Source-anchored exact-lag adaptation
91    3.1   Exact-lag parent training

92    For each training domain, the data loader reads all ﬁve 450 K replicas. Starting-state cluster labels
93    are used only to balance sampling: clusters are sampled uniformly, and an equal number of observed
94    starts is drawn from each selected cluster with replacement when required. Each sampled pair is
95    taken from one replica and separated by exactly 50 frames. Thus direct endpoint supervision cannot
96    cross replica boundaries or substitute a Markov-state draw for the observed future.
97    The seed-1 parent begins from the source checkpoint and updates all 34,152,521 parameters. It
98    trains for 10 epochs, 160 optimizer updates, and 30,720 sampled transitions with batch size 8 and
99    learning rate 3 × 10−6 . A ﬁxed validation transition population is materialized once, and ﬂow-
100   matching randomness is replayed so validation losses are paired across epochs. The best validation
101   checkpoint is selected by minimum validation loss. This optimization took 121.80 seconds on one
102   NVIDIA H100 and peaked at 64,684,237,824 allocated CUDA bytes. These are measurements of
103   the selected training run, not the total search campaign and not a hardware-independent efﬁciency
104   claim.

105   3.2   Source anchoring

106   Let θ0 denote the source weights and θp the ﬁne-tuned parent. The evaluated candidate is
                                    θα = θ0 + α(θp − θ0 ),        α = 0.0735.                              (1)
107   Floating tensors are interpolated in their stored dtype; non-ﬂoating state must match exactly. No
108   additional gradient update is performed after interpolation. The selected checkpoint therefore retains
109   7.35% of the parent’s learned displacement and uses a single model at inference.
110   The coefﬁcient is a development-selected operating point, not a physically meaningful parameter.
111   A nearby 7.5% anchor failed the frozen one-protein retention pilot, whereas 7.35% passed. This
112   narrow boundary argues against interpreting the coefﬁcient as generally reusable. It also illustrates
113   why endpoint improvement alone is insufﬁcient: the source distance must be considered jointly with
114   explicitly named retention observables.

127   We report improvement as baseline error minus candidate error, so positive values favor the can-
128   didate. Protein-level means and two-sided 95% percentile intervals use 100,000 bootstrap resam-
129   ples with proteins as the resampling unit. The fraction improved is the fraction of protein-level
130   differences strictly above zero. Source and candidate receipts are rejected if persistence values or
131   transition indices differ.

132   4.2   Compact training replication

133   Two additional parents repeat the same ten-epoch training and 7.35% interpolation with independent
134   training seeds. Each is evaluated on the same eight-protein compact panel with all ﬁve replicas, four
135   starts per replica, four samples per start, exact lag 50, and ten ODE steps. Because the panel is
136   the same previously inspected validation subset, the replication probes optimization-seed stability
137   within this development regime; it does not establish population-level external generalization.

138   4.3   Ensemble-retention protocol

139   The retention suite compares seed-1 candidate and source on eight validation proteins. For each pro-
140   tein and checkpoint, the ofﬁcial breadth-ﬁrst generation tree uses breadth 200, retains 500 samples,
141   runs in chunks of 100, uses 50 ODE steps, and starts from the same frame and seed. References use
142   all available frames from all ﬁve 450 K replicas. Generated samples are unordered ensemble draws;
143   the tree is not interpreted as a temporal trajectory.
144   Seven observables are evaluated after common-frame Cα alignment: dynamic cross-correlation
145   matrix (DCCM) mean absolute error, native-contact-fraction Wasserstein-1 distance, pairwise-
146   RMSD Wasserstein-1 distance, PCA-subspace RMSIP, prediction clash fraction, radius-of-gyration
147   Wasserstein-1 distance, and RMSF RMSE. For lower-is-better metrics, degradation is candidate mi-
148   nus source; for RMSIP, degradation is source minus candidate. Negative values therefore favor the
149   candidate in every row.
150   The gate was deﬁned before the aggregate. For each observable, the upper endpoint of the protein-
151   bootstrap interval for mean degradation must not exceed its mean-degradation limit, at least six
152   of eight proteins must be within that limit, and no protein may exceed twice the limit. All seven
153   observables must pass. This is a non-inferiority-style development screen with ﬁxed tolerances, not
154   a statistical equivalence test and not evidence that the generated ensemble is physically correct.

155   5 Results
156   5.1   Complete eligible endpoint panel

157   Table 1 gives the primary comparisons. Protein-macro ensemble-mean RMSD is 11.2602 Å for
158   source and 11.2276 Å for the candidate. Their difference is 0.03259 Å with interval [0.02525,
159   0.04011], and 27/28 proteins improve. The candidate also improves over coordinate persistence,
160   whose matched protein-macro error is 11.6640 Å, by 0.43641 Å with interval [0.20303, 0.68065];
161   22/28 proteins improve.
162   The source comparison is the informative measure of adaptation because both models share archi-
163   tecture and inference. Its absolute gain is small relative to the roughly 11 Å endpoint errors in this
164   high-temperature, 50-frame task. The persistence comparison conﬁrms that the selected sampling
165   procedure is better than returning the start on average, but persistence is a weak baseline and does
166   not establish competitiveness with other learned simulators.

167   5.2   Training-seed replication

168   On the compact panel, the three source gains are 0.03009, 0.02291, and 0.02901 Å, for a mean of
169   0.02734 Å across parents. Every one of the eight proteins improves over source for each parent,
170   and each protein-bootstrap interval excludes zero (Table 1). The spread is small on the scale of
171   the primary errors, but it is non-negligible relative to the effect itself: seed 2 yields about three
172   quarters of the seed-1 gain. Accordingly, the evidence supports repeatability of a small positive
173   compact-panel effect, not invariance to training randomness.

174   5.3   Retention observables

175   All seven retention observables pass their frozen rules (Table 2). Six mean degradations are negative
176   or close to zero; PCA RMSIP and native-contact Wasserstein show small positive mean degradation
177   but remain below their ﬁxed limits. The interval for radius-of-gyration Wasserstein lies below zero,
178   while several other intervals straddle zero. These signs are descriptive. Passing means that each
179   aggregate interval, within-limit fraction, and worst-protein value satisﬁed the predeclared thresholds;
180   it does not prove equality of source and candidate ensembles.


181   6 Negative evidence and interpretation

182   The selected result emerged from a failure-sensitive sequence. A fully exact-lag parent, output-head-
183   only adaptations, and 25% and 50% mixtures of exact-lag and source objectives improved transition
184   prediction more than conservative source anchors in earlier screens, yet failed frozen equilibrium-
185   retention limits. An MSM-only control retained ensemble behavior but did not show a protein-
186   bootstrap-supported endpoint improvement. Among earlier anchors, 10% missed one retention limit
187   and 20% missed multiple limits. These outcomes rule out the simple strategy of maximizing exact-
188   lag validation improvement without a separate retention constraint.
189   The present sweep also contains a close negative control: a 7.5% interpolation failed the one-protein
190   equilibrium pilot, whereas 7.35% passed. This 0.15-percentage-point boundary is too ﬁne to sup-
191   port a general prescription. It instead exposes sensitivity to development selection and motivates
192   evaluating anchor coefﬁcients jointly on larger, untouched retention cohorts in future work.
193   Finally, the present candidate is not uniformly superior to the earlier anchored checkpoint under
194   every training seed. In seed-matched compact comparisons, two seeds favor the new candidate,
195   while seed 2 differs by −0.00110 Å and its protein-bootstrap interval crosses zero. Averaged over
196   the three seeds the new conﬁguration is modestly better, but the seed-2 result prevents a claim of
197   uniform dominance. Reporting this comparison is important because the new candidate’s consistent
198   advantage over the public source does not imply consistent advantage over every nearby develop-
199   ment checkpoint.
200   Taken together, the evidence supports a workﬂow-level lesson: controlling source distance can pre-
201   serve a transition gain that survives predeﬁned ensemble screens. It does not show that linear inter-
202   polation is optimal, that the chosen coefﬁcient transfers to another dataset, or that passing source-
203   relative observables ensures physical validity. The retention suite protects selected properties of the
204   source; any systematic source error can be inherited.

205   7 Limitations and next evidence
206   Validation reuse. The strongest limitation is selection and evaluation on previously inspected val-
207   idation data. The complete 28-protein panel is exhaustive for the frozen length-eligible validation
208   population, but it is not untouched. Bootstrap intervals describe variation among these proteins, not
209   post-selection coverage for a new population. Standard-test access remains zero.

210   One lag and one condition. All supervised and scored transitions use exactly one 50-frame sepa-
211   ration at 450 K. There is no multi-lag consistency test, semigroup test, replica-level kinetic estimate,
212   calibrated physical time, rate, rare-event evaluation, or path-conditioning experiment. Endpoints are
213   ﬁnite-window Cα samples, not molecular trajectories.

214   Restricted proteins and coordinates. The endpoint panel contains 28 mdCATH validation do-
215   mains no longer than 256 residues. The experiment does not establish behavior for longer domains,
216   other temperatures, other molecular classes, unseen experimental structures, ligands, environments,
217   or sequence families outside this panel. Scoring is Cα based. The clash statistic and the seven reten-
218   tion observables do not validate all-atom stereochemistry, side-chain packing, energy, or chemistry.

219   Limited baselines. Only the pinned source and coordinate persistence are matched. Persistence is
220   informative as a no-motion control but weak as a ﬁeld comparator. Without runnable learned base-
221   lines under identical starts, stochastic sample count, alignment, and split, no ﬁeld-competitiveness
222   conclusion is available.

223   Retention is source-relative. The equilibrium screen asks whether the candidate stays within
224   ﬁxed degradation limits relative to source on eight proteins. The limits are not equivalence mar-
225   gins derived from biological utility, and the suite uses only the seed-1 checkpoint. Passing cannot
226   establish molecular equilibrium, correct Boltzmann weights, or correct kinetics. It also cannot detect
227   properties absent from the seven observables.

228   Small effect and incomplete seed coverage. The source gain is 0.03259 Å against source error
229   11.2602 Å. Three compact training replications are encouraging, but the full 28-protein endpoint
230   panel and equilibrium suite were not repeated for every seed. The neighboring-anchor failures and
231   the seed-2 comparison to an earlier anchor show that selection uncertainty remains material.
232   The next defensible sequence is therefore: preregister multi-lag and replica-sensitive validation on a
233   fresh boundary; add matched runnable ﬁeld baselines; expand ensemble and geometry checks; and
234   open the standard test partition exactly once only after those gates pass. None of those experiments
235   is reported here.

252   9 Conclusion
253   A 7.35% source-anchored interpolation of a ten-epoch exact-lag parent preserves a small, repeatable
254   improvement over source M AR S-FM on previously inspected mdCATH validation proteins while
255   passing a separate seven-observable retention screen. The result is useful as a bounded adaptation
256   study because it includes nearby failures, seed variability, and explicit evidence controls. Its scope
257   remains narrow: one 50-frame lag, 450 K, Cα endpoints, validation only, two matched baselines, and
258   no opened standard-test records. The appropriate conclusion is that conservative weight anchoring
259   merits further preregistered evaluation—not that molecular dynamics, kinetics, or general protein
260   behavior has been solved.

281   B Additional interpretation of retention metrics
282   The retention suite intentionally spans correlated motion (DCCM), ﬂuctuation magnitude (RMSF),
283   global size (radius of gyration), distributional spread (pairwise RMSD), low-dimensional covariance
284   geometry (RMSIP), contact preservation, and a coarse Cα clash statistic. These quantities are com-
285   plementary but incomplete. For example, similar RMSF can coexist with different cross-correlations,
286   and similar radius-of-gyration distributions can coexist with different local contacts. Requiring ev-
287   ery observable to pass avoids averaging a large regression in one property against an improvement in
288   another. The remaining gaps listed in Section 7 are not ﬁlled by adding these observables together.
